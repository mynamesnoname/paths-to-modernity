# WP-012 — Implement fuzzy predicates and the argument evaluator

## Objective

Calibrate graded predicates and combine them into reproducible argument strengths without replacing hard physical constraints.

## Planned work

- Membership functions
- Weighted argument evaluator
- Calibration configuration and report

## Decisions

- 2026-10-10 — **Parameters are justified, not blindly fitted.** The initial
  predicate parameters come from thresholds already present in
  `single_hypothesis_redrock_skill.md`, `classification.md`, and
  `hypothesis_synthesis_skill.md`.
- 2026-10-10 — **Correlated evidence is grouped by ontology concept.** Multiple
  arguments about the same concept are averaged before summing so redundant
  claims cannot outvote independent evidence.

  missing-line guard conditions.

## Review and semantic correction 2026-10-12

The decision owner clarified the intended meaning of HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW:
these are **ordinal ratings of defects found in a redshift hypothesis**,
not estimates of its probability of being correct. HIGH means no material
defect was found within checks actually performed; MEDIUM means limited or
non-fatal concerns; LOW means severe or accumulated defects. An unavailable
check is not evidence that no defect exists. The exact handling of insufficient
assessment coverage must be specified in the output contract before grading
such a case; this review does not silently add a fourth grade.

This supersedes the *acceptance interpretation* in the earlier reviews and
the 2026-10-12 completion claim, not the historical frozen experiment:

- `evaluation/calibration/SPEC.md`, `calibration_parameters_frozen.json`,
  and `evaluator_calibration_report_v2.{json,md}` remain immutable historical
  descriptions of the 12-tune/20-evaluate run. They measure coarse-type and
  redshift correctness among evaluable outputs, exclusions, and the ECE
  obtained by assigning nominal correctness probabilities of 0.8/0.5/0.2 to
  the grades. Since the grades do not claim those probabilities, that ECE is
  **not** a validation metric for the intended defect rating.
- The `decisive_correct` selection rule counts a correct LOW as success.
  That is not evidence that a LOW defect rating is justified; nor is a
  correct LOW automatically an error. Six of seven evaluable held-out
  outputs were correct and graded LOW, while ten runs abstained and three
  produced no artifacts. Keep those observations as diagnostics, not a
  `7/7` defect-grade acceptance result or class-wide performance claim.
- Example: BGS/69 is type/redshift-correct but its frame carries seven
  support and eleven full-weight missing-line attacks, yielding score -1.0
  and LOW. The current guard uses coverage plus *spectrum-median* SNR >= 1.5;
  it does not prove each absent line should be detectable at its own
  wavelength. Review the eleven attacks individually before deciding whether
  LOW correctly describes real defects or over-penalizes this hypothesis.

Remaining acceptance work:

1. Freeze a grade/output contract: grade definitions, severity and
   accumulation rules, what counts as an assessed check, treatment of
   missing/unknown evidence, and evidence-linked reasons. Distinguish any
   internal severity score from a correctness probability and from the
   pipeline's present final-report confidence field.
2. Verify invariants with targeted cases: unknown or unobservable missing
   lines stay neutral; adding a valid defect cannot improve the grade;
   severe physical contradictions cannot be canceled solely by many weak
   supports. Audit per-line detectability and the current -1.0 score
   saturation on correct hypotheses.
3. Compare frozen grades with independent, preferably blinded expert defect
   assessments on an appropriate held-out set. Report agreement by grade,
   severe-defect misses, disagreements, assessment coverage, abstentions,
   failures, and sample counts. Report classification/redshift correctness
   separately. No numeric acceptance threshold is invented by this review.
   Because the existing 20 evaluation outputs and scores have already been
   inspected under a different target, use them only for retrospective
   diagnosis; freeze a new evaluation boundary before claiming an independent
   revised-grade validation. Existing run artifacts may be reused where
   independence of annotation and rule selection is demonstrable.

Status returns to `in_progress` until the revised defect-grade contract and
its evidence are accepted. This review does not rerun FITS or LLM tests.

## Independent rating validation 2026-10-14 (boundary and instrument)

The remaining acceptance clause is the one the 2026-10-12 review left open:
compare the frozen grades with **independent, blinded** defect-severity
assessments on a **newly frozen** boundary, and report agreement, severe-defect
misses, coverage, abstentions, failures and sample counts — with correctness and
probability calibration on separate axes and no invented pass threshold.

**Boundary (revision 1, frozen before any assessment).**
`evaluation/calibration/rating_validation_split.json`: 16 spectra, 4 per class,
selected deterministically under the `forma-wp012-rating-validation-20261014-v1`
seed, each with `VI_QUALITY >= 3.5` **and** DESI's own `quality == "high"`, whose
`TARGETID` appears neither in `example/basic` nor in any `labeled_split.json`
entry, with the CSV label cross-checked against the FITS `FIBERMAP`. The selector
(`select_rating_validation_split.py`) reads labels only — never a FORMA output —
so the boundary is independent of every rating, rule selection and score inspected
so far. Revision 1 is recorded in the protocol: the first boundary's first smoke
spectrum stopped on the fast-mode tool budget because it has almost no detectable
feature, and the unit of analysis here is *the hypothesis the pipeline selected and
rated*, so the revision adds the DESI quality flag as an **evaluability**
criterion. Its bias (brighter spectra; the result does not generalize to
featureless or low-SNR spectra) and the run-once rule are both recorded there. No
assessment or agreement number existed when the revision was made.

**Instrument** (`evaluation/calibration/defect_annotation.py`,
`VALIDATION_PROTOCOL.md`): `packets` builds one blinded packet per unit and
mechanically refuses to emit the rating, the auditor verdict, the fatal cap, the
rule findings or the analyst's own prose — the allow-list is exactly the
protocol's visible list; `form` renders a fill-in form; `score` computes the
pre-fixed metrics. `tests/reasoning/test_defect_annotation.py` (14 tests) pins
blinding (including an injected forbidden key and "the packet is identical with
and without the auditor verdict"), the metric arithmetic (misses,
over-penalisation, unrated units, unassessed units, correctness as a separate
axis), the boundary's determinism and disjointness, and the protocol's recorded
non-decisions. A labelled retrospective dry run
(the retrospective dry run, since removed in the 2026-10-15 cleanup, recorded
`acceptance_evidence: false` and showed
the packet builder and the scorer working end to end on the 20 superseded
evaluate runs: 20 packets, 17 rated / 3 unrated, 13 both-null on the correctness
axis, agreement matrix empty by construction because no assessment exists for
those units.

**A real-workflow defect found and fixed while executing the boundary.**
`.test_set/DESI/BGS/fits_input/54.fits` produced zero emission features; the CWT
finder returned early instead of writing an empty catalog, and the evidence-graph
builder — which requires both catalogs — aborted the whole analysis under
`EVIDENCE_GRAPH=true`. `cwt_feature_finder._save_csv` now always writes both
catalogs (header-only when a kind has no features), with the empty frame's derived
columns guarded so the header keeps the schema its readers validate against;
`tests/evidence/test_cwt_catalog_artifacts.py` pins both properties. The defect was
in the deterministic layer (`WP-004`'s file) and is recorded here because this
package's execution exposed it; `WP-037` should re-verify it on the real-workflow
gate.

**Execution.** The 16 units are run **once** each through `scripts/main.py`
(Redrock, the model stages, the graph projection) by
`evaluation/calibration/run_rating_validation.py`, which records secret-free
provenance per spectrum (source digest, configuration fingerprint, model, git
head, split digest, duration, artifact inventory) in
`rating_validation_runlog.json`. A failure, abstention or budget stop is a recorded
outcome, never a reason to rerun that spectrum.

**Outstanding.** The assessments themselves. The acceptance clause requires
*independent* defect-severity judgements; the decision owner is the domain expert
who provides them, from the blinded packets, and this package does not substitute
an agent's own judgement for that (the protocol explicitly forbids pooling an
`llm-surrogate` record with an expert's). Once the completed assessments exist,
`score` produces the report and this section will carry its numbers.

abort, or fewer tool rounds per line).

## SH tool-rule revision result 2026-10-15 (revision 3)

The decision owner's SH tool-calling rules cut the cost of a run by roughly a
factor of three — median 46 tool rounds and 145 s against revision 2's 210 rounds —
and removed the budget failures entirely (0 of 16, against 1). They also cut the
**ratable** output from 8 units to **3** (`BGS/6 MEDIUM`, `BGS/93 HIGH`,
`QSO/1 MEDIUM`); 12 runs abstained with `no_candidate` and one failed.

The cause is a single interaction, and it is worth fixing rather than living with:

- `harness/tools.py::write_lines_csv` **raises** when a row is fit-derived
  (`source` is `fit_peak`/`fit_doublet`) and also carries `ridge_length`/`cwt_snr`
  — the rule the owner added, and a correct rule: a fitted centre must not claim
  CWT metrics.
- Under the new SH rules the agent produces exactly that row shape routinely, and
  the raise destroys the **whole hypothesis**: 12 of the 16 boundary runs logged
  `cannot carry CWT ridge_length or cwt_snr` (23 occurrences, 1-5 hypotheses each).
- When every hypothesis in a run loses its catalog, `hypothesis_synthesis/catalog.csv`
  is written header-only, the synthesis has no candidate and the run records
  `no_candidate`. The correlation is exact: the only three runs with a non-empty
  catalog (6, 93, 1 — 16/22/23 rows) are the only three rated units.

A repairable tool error (or nulling the CWT fields with a recorded note) would let
one malformed row cost one row instead of the analysis. Until that is decided, the
assessment form carries the three rated units and the report records the 13
unratable ones.

One further failure mode was observed once: `BGS/80` aborted because the
FeatureAuditor skips with `No LIKELY or MARGINAL features to verify` and the

**Two tooling defects were found and fixed while running this cohort**, both in
this package's calibration tooling:

1. **Run directories were not namespaced by class.** A FITS index is unique only
   within its class, so a flat `{root}/{index}` layout merged `BGS/3.fits` with
   `ELG/3.fits`: the second class was skipped as "already complete" while the
   first class's run sat in the shared directory. This cohort collided on 9 of its
   16 entries and the first pass was discarded as invalid. The pipeline is not at
   fault — one invocation of `scripts/main.py` writes `OUTPUT_DIR/{FILE_NAME}` as
   documented, and earlier cohorts never collided — so the correction is entirely
   in this package's harness: `defect_annotation.run_directory()` resolves
   `{root}/{class}/{index}` (with a read-only fallback to archived flat roots) and
   the driver gives each class its own `OUTPUT_DIR`. `scripts/main.py` and the
   pipeline are untouched by this fix. The frozen rating-validation split has no
   collisions, so its earlier results are unaffected.
   **Identity is now verified, not assumed**: the spectrum node records
   `input_fits_sha256`, so `verify_identity()` compares a run directory against the
   split's own digest. The packets builder refuses a directory holding another
   spectrum (`identity_mismatch`) instead of asking an assessor to judge the wrong
   spectrum, and the driver re-executes rather than reporting a foreign run as
   `already_complete`. Five tests pin the separation, the refusal and the driver's
   three-way verdict; the challenge comparison reports 16/16 units identity-matched.
2. **Output roots must be inside the repository.** The evidence-graph builders
   refuse artifacts that cannot be referenced repo-relatively, so a run root under
   `/tmp` fails in the visual-interpreter stage. Runs live under `.data/runs/`.
